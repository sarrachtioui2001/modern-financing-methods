00 · Why modern financing?

Classic financing has limits

Classic sources

What companies used

  • Self-financing
  • Bank loans
  • Capital increase
  • Bonds (large firms only)
The limits

Why young firms are refused

  • Banks ask for collateral
  • No track record of accounts
  • Intangible assets are hard to pledge
  • Slow, costly procedures

Modern methods fill this gap: they finance young, small and innovative companies.

Classic financing means self-financing, bank loans, a capital increase, or bonds for large companies. [click] But a young company is often refused: the bank asks for collateral, the company has no history of accounts, its value is in intangible assets like software or a brand, and the process is slow. [click] Modern methods were created exactly for this gap.

01 · Crowdfunding

Financing by the crowd

Many people each give a small amount through an online platform.

Donationnothing in return
Rewarda product (pre-sale)
Lendingrepaid with interest
Equityshares of the firm

Advantages

  • No collateral needed
  • Tests the market first

Limits

  • Mostly small amounts
  • Idea exposed publicly

Tunisia: crowdfunding law n° 2020-37, platforms supervised by the CMF.

Crowdfunding: many people each give a small amount through an online platform. [click] The money flows from the crowd to the platform, which keeps a fee, and then to the project. [click] There are four types: donation, reward, which is really a pre-sale, lending with interest, and equity, where the crowd receives shares. [click] Its strengths: no collateral, and it tests the market before launching. Its limits: the amounts are usually small, and the idea is public. [click] In Tunisia, a crowdfunding law was adopted in 2020 and the platforms are supervised by the CMF. (VERIFY the law number and the licensed platforms on cmf.tn.)

02 · Private equity

Business angels and venture capital

Investors buy a share of the company and earn when they sell it later.

Advantages

  • Large amounts, no repayment
  • Advice and contacts

Limits

  • Founders lose shares
  • Only high-growth projects

Tunisia: venture capital through SICAR companies.

Private equity: investors buy a share of the company, and they earn money when they sell that share later. [click] A young company first loses cash: this is the valley of death. [click] At each stage a different investor steps in: business angels at the seed stage, venture capital funds for the start-up, growth capital later, and finally an LBO or a stock market listing. [click] It brings large amounts with no repayment, plus advice. But the founders give away part of their company, and investors only choose projects that can grow fast. [click] In Tunisia, venture capital goes through companies called SICAR. (VERIFY.)

03 · Leasing (crédit-bail)

Rent the asset, buy it later

A leasing company buys the equipment and rents it to the firm.

Advantages

  • Up to 100 % financed
  • The asset is the guarantee

Limits

  • Costs more than a loan
  • Not the owner until the end

End of contract: purchase option, return or renew. Tunisia: e.g. Tunisie Leasing et Factoring, Hannibal Lease.

Leasing, or crédit-bail. [click] The company chooses its equipment. [click] The leasing company buys it from the supplier, [click] the supplier delivers it to the company, [click] and the company pays a monthly rent. [click] It can finance up to 100 percent of the asset, and the asset itself is the guarantee. But in total it costs more than a loan, and the company is not the owner during the contract. [click] At the end, the company can buy the asset for a small residual price, return it, or renew. In Tunisia, several listed leasing companies offer this. (VERIFY names.)

04 · Factoring (affacturage)

Turn invoices into cash today

The company sells its unpaid invoices to a factor, who pays now.

Invoice10,000 TND Interest: 10,000 × 9 % × 60 / 360− 150 Factoring commission (1 %)− 100 Cash received today9,750 TND

Advantages

  • Immediate cash, lower BFR
  • The factor collects

Limits

  • Costly
  • Only for B2B sales
Factoring, or affacturage. [click] The company sells to a customer who will pay in 60 days. [click] Instead of waiting, it sells the invoice to a factor, [click] who pays it now, [click] and the customer pays the factor at the due date. [click] A quick example: an invoice of 10,000 dinars, 60 days, 9 percent a year: 150 dinars of interest, plus a 1 percent commission, so the company receives 9,750 dinars today. These figures are illustrative. [click] It gives immediate cash and reduces the working capital need, the BFR. But it is expensive, and it only works for sales to other businesses.

05 · Microfinance

Small loans for the excluded

Credit for people and micro-firms that banks do not serve.

For whom?

Micro-entrepreneurs

Artisans, farmers, small traders, people with no bank access.

How?

Small, short, close

Small amounts, short terms, little or no collateral, regular follow-up.

Trade-off

Easy access, higher rate

Interest rates are higher than bank loans, and amounts are limited.

Tunisia: institutions supervised by the ACM, e.g. Enda Tamweel; also the BTS.

Microfinance gives credit to people and micro-businesses that banks do not serve: artisans, farmers, small traders. [click] The loans are small and short, with little or no collateral, and a close follow-up of the borrower. [click] The trade-off: access is easy, but the interest rate is higher and the amounts are limited. [click] In Tunisia, microfinance institutions are supervised by the ACM; Enda Tamweel is the best known, and the BTS also lends to small projects. (VERIFY.)

06 · Islamic finance

Financing backed by real assets

No speculationno gharar
Shared riskprofit and loss
Real assetbehind every deal
InstrumentHow it worksClassic equivalent
MurabahaThe bank buys the asset and resells it with a known marginInvestment loan
IjaraThe bank buys the asset and rents itLeasing
MusharakaBank and firm invest together, share profit and lossPrivate equity
SukukCertificates of ownership in an income-producing assetBonds

Tunisia: e.g. Banque Zitouna, Al Baraka Bank, Wifak International Bank.

Islamic finance follows four principles: no interest, no excessive uncertainty or speculation, sharing of profit and loss, and a real asset behind every operation. [click] In practice: Murabaha, where the bank buys an asset and resells it with a known margin, close to an investment loan. Ijara, which is Islamic leasing. Musharaka, a partnership close to private equity. And sukuk, close to bonds, but backed by real assets. [click] In Tunisia, several banks offer these products. (VERIFY names.)

07 · Digital finance

Fintech changes the access

Online lending

Credit in days

Decisions based on data instead of collateral.

Mobile payment

E-wallets

Faster collection, fewer cash problems.

Revenue-based

Repaid from sales

A share of monthly revenue, no fixed instalment.

Blockchain

Tokens

Digital tokens that represent shares or assets.

Advantages

  • Fast, online, 24/7
  • Reaches the unbanked

Limits

  • Regulation still evolving
  • Cyber and crypto risk

Tunisia: BCT regulatory sandbox for fintech; e-Dinar by La Poste.

Finally, digital finance. Online lenders decide in days using data instead of collateral. [click] Mobile payment and e-wallets speed up cash collection. [click] Revenue-based financing is repaid as a share of monthly sales. [click] And blockchain tokens can represent shares or assets. [click] It is fast and reaches people outside banks, but the regulation is still evolving and the risks are new. [click] In Tunisia, the central bank runs a regulatory sandbox for fintech, and La Poste offers the e-Dinar. (VERIFY.)

Conclusion

Key takeaways

  1. Modern methods finance companies that banks avoid.
  2. Each method fits a specific need: an asset, invoices, growth.
  3. They complement bank loans rather than replace them.
  4. Digital and Islamic finance are growing fast, including in Tunisia.
